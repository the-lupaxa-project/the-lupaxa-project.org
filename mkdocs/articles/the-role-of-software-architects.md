# Why Software Architects Should Focus on Architecture, Not Code

In software development, the role of a software architect is critical for ensuring that projects are built on a solid, scalable, and sustainable foundation. While many software architects have extensive coding backgrounds and deep technical expertise, their role as architects is fundamentally different from that of a developer. By focusing exclusively on high-level design and architectural decisions, rather than implementation, architects can provide the guidance, strategic oversight, and long-term planning necessary to align a project's structure with business goals.

This article explores the reasons why software architects should prioritize architecture over coding, the risks of overlapping roles, and the value of architects maintaining a holistic view of the project estate.

## 1. Architects as Visionaries: Focusing on the Big Picture

A key role of the software architect is to develop and maintain a comprehensive vision of the system. This involves understanding business requirements, identifying technical constraints, and planning for scalability, maintainability, and security. By staying at a high level, architects can ensure that each component of the system aligns with the larger vision and that all technical decisions support long-term goals.

### Why Big-Picture Focus is Important:

- **Alignment with Business Goals:** Architects work closely with stakeholders to understand business objectives and translate these into technical requirements. Their focus on architecture ensures that the entire system is built with these objectives in mind.
- **Avoiding Technical Debt:** Architects are responsible for setting standards that minimize technical debt, ensuring that the system remains adaptable and scalable as it evolves. By staying focused on architecture rather than getting drawn into coding, architects can make high-level decisions that reduce the need for extensive refactoring in the future.
- **Consistency Across Components:** When an architect is solely focused on architecture, they can ensure that every component fits into a cohesive whole. This is particularly important in complex systems, where maintaining consistency across different parts of the codebase can prevent miscommunication and integration issues.

Architects act as visionaries, guiding the development process in alignment with long-term business goals. Shifting their focus to coding could compromise this high-level perspective, reducing the overall coherence and effectiveness of the design.

## 2. Ensuring a Steady Flow of Designs

In a development team, architects are often the bottleneck when it comes to delivering high-level designs. Their role requires thoughtful planning, and their output is crucial for developers to begin implementing new features or components. If architects divide their time between designing and coding, they may not be able to deliver the continuous flow of design work that engineers need to keep moving forward.

### Benefits of a Design-Only Focus:

- **Maintaining Momentum:** Engineers rely on architects to provide a steady flow of designs and specifications. If architects spend too much time coding, they may delay critical designs, slowing down the entire team.
- **Avoiding Role Confusion:** Architects can best serve their teams by producing design artifacts, such as architecture diagrams, component specifications, and interface definitions. These documents provide a roadmap for engineers, ensuring that each member of the team has a clear understanding of what they're building and how it fits into the larger system.
- **Rapid Adaptation to Changes:** Architectural needs can shift quickly as business priorities evolve. An architect focused solely on architecture can more readily respond to these changes and adjust designs without being tied down by specific coding tasks.

By concentrating exclusively on producing and refining designs, architects provide a consistent, reliable foundation for engineers to implement and build upon. This separation of roles allows the team to maintain productivity and reduce bottlenecks.

## 3. Maintaining a Holistic View of the System

Architects are responsible for maintaining a holistic view of the entire system. They must understand how each part of the system interacts with others, identify dependencies, and foresee potential issues. When architects start coding, they may become too focused on specific implementation details, losing sight of the overall architecture and its interdependencies.

### Advantages of the Holistic View:

- **Inter-Component Coordination:** A key part of the architect's role is managing how different components interact and ensuring that the system functions seamlessly as a whole. By keeping a high-level perspective, architects can better manage these interactions and prevent issues that arise when components are designed in isolation.
- **Security and Compliance:** Architects are often responsible for ensuring that the system meets security and compliance requirements. When they maintain a holistic view, they're better positioned to implement security measures at the architectural level, rather than as afterthoughts in the code.
- **Long-Term Planning:** Architects must constantly think about the system's long-term maintainability and scalability. By focusing on architecture instead of coding, they can make decisions that allow the system to grow and adapt to future needs without requiring extensive rework.

With a full estate view, architects can identify risks, avoid design inconsistencies, and make informed decisions that optimize system performance and stability. Coding, with its detail-oriented focus, can distract architects from maintaining this critical high-level perspective.

## 4. Preserving Objectivity and Avoiding Micro-Optimization

When architects get involved in coding, they risk becoming too focused on implementation specifics and optimization. This can lead to micro-optimization, where architects may prioritize improvements at the code level rather than at the architectural level, which may not align with the system's overall goals. Architects should maintain objectivity, focusing on structural improvements rather than the minutiae of individual code blocks.

### Importance of Architectural Objectivity:

- **Focus on Strategic Optimizations:** Architects should focus on optimizations that benefit the entire system, such as choosing the right architectural pattern or ensuring efficient data flow. Getting involved in coding can lead to focusing on isolated optimizations that may not significantly impact system performance.
- **Consistent Decision-Making:** Architects provide direction and consistency across the team, and their decisions affect multiple developers. By staying at a high level, architects can make balanced choices without getting caught up in specific coding challenges or preferences.
- **Minimizing Bias:** When architects write code, they may develop a bias toward specific implementations or design patterns that are easier to code. By staying out of the code, architects remain objective and can evaluate design choices based on their broader impact on the system, not just individual components.

By focusing on architecture rather than implementation, architects can ensure that their recommendations are balanced and made with the system's long-term health and scalability in mind.

## 5. Enhancing Communication and Collaboration

An architect who isn't tied to coding tasks is better positioned to act as a bridge between developers and stakeholders. Architects play a crucial role in translating technical requirements into actionable design plans, coordinating with team leads, and presenting solutions to stakeholders. By avoiding coding tasks, architects can dedicate more time to communicating the rationale behind design decisions and ensuring that the team remains aligned.

### Benefits of Communication-Centric Roles:

- **Clear Documentation and Guidance:** Architects who focus on design are better able to document their decisions and provide developers with clear implementation guidelines. This documentation is invaluable for onboarding, knowledge transfer, and future maintenance.
- **Collaborative Problem-Solving:** Architects serve as advisors to developers, providing guidance on implementing complex solutions. When architects are fully available, they can collaborate with developers on design challenges, ensuring that solutions align with the architectural vision.
- **Stakeholder Engagement:** Architects work closely with stakeholders to understand evolving business needs and ensure that the architecture supports future requirements. By staying out of the code, architects have more availability for strategic discussions and can advocate for architectural changes that benefit the business.

When architects focus exclusively on architecture, they have the time and bandwidth needed to facilitate smooth communication across the team, which is essential for maintaining a shared understanding of the project's direction.

## 6. Mitigating Role Confusion and Avoiding Bottlenecks

Finally, when architects take on coding responsibilities, it can lead to role confusion and create bottlenecks in the development process. Developers may become uncertain about who is responsible for specific aspects of the project, and the architect's coding responsibilities can cause delays in delivering designs.

### Advantages of Role Clarity:

- **Efficient Division of Labour:** When architects focus on design and developers on implementation, everyone knows their responsibilities, which improves efficiency and accountability.
- **Preventing Bottlenecks:** Architects can become a bottleneck if they're trying to balance coding with design work. By focusing solely on architecture, they can provide design deliverables more consistently, enabling developers to implement without unnecessary delays.
- **Clear Decision Hierarchy:** Having architects focus on high-level decisions and developers on implementation ensures that each team member's expertise is leveraged appropriately. This division also helps developers know where to turn for guidance on design versus implementation issues.

Maintaining distinct roles allows architects and developers to work more effectively and ensures a smooth workflow without overlapping responsibilities.

## Conclusion: The Architect's Essential Role in Designing, Not Coding

A software architect's role is distinct from that of a developer and requires a unique focus on design, strategy, and long-term planning. Architects who concentrate solely on architecture ensure that the project is built on a solid foundation that aligns with business goals, remains scalable, and provides a consistent framework for developers to build upon. By staying out of the code, architects maintain the big-picture perspective, deliver timely designs, and support effective collaboration and communication across the team.

When architects focus on architecture, they allow developers to thrive in their implementation roles and the team to move forward with confidence. The resulting structure is a more cohesive, well-designed system that not only meets immediate requirements but is also adaptable to future needs.
